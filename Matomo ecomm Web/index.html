Statutory Compliance FAQs

Authoritative guidance on your legal rights, exemptions, and timelines.

As a Data Principal, Section 12(1)(d) empowers you to demand the complete erasure of your personal data when consent is withdrawn or when the purpose for which it was collected is satisfied. Upon submission, the Data Fiduciary and all downstream third-party processors are mandated to delete your records.

All direct identifiers (customer name, email address, password hash, saved delivery addresses, wishlist items) and pseudonymous telemetry (Matomo tracking profiles, Google Analytics identifiers, advertising preferences, and cookie tokens) are permanently destroyed.

Under Section 12(3) of the Act, erasure does not apply where retention is necessary for the performance of a contract or statutory compliance. If an order is currently in transit or within its return/warranty window, data essential to fulfill the shipment and protect consumer dispute rights is retained until completed, after which erasure is automatically finalized.

Section 36 of the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) Act, 2017 and Section 44AA of the Indian Income Tax Act mandate that businesses preserve sales accounts and tax invoices for up to 72 months (6 years). These records are strictly archived in an isolated, encrypted accounting vault and are never utilized for marketing or analytics.

In accordance with DPDP implementing rules, the Data Fiduciary must verify identity and complete erasure within a statutory window of 30 calendar days. You will receive an official audit summary confirming the action.

You may directly address formal statutory notices to our Data Protection Officer at dpo@auraandblush.com. Under Section 13, if a grievance is unresolved within 30 days, you retain the legal right to escalate the complaint to the Data Protection Board of India.
